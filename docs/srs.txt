1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the complete set of requirements for a multi-restaurant food delivery marketplace for Cameroon, launching in Buea (working title: Resto, referred to in this document as “the Platform”). It consolidates into a single document the requirements that were first written separately for the four actors identified so far — the Customer, the Merchant (restaurant owner or manager), the Delivery Agent (motorcycle rider) and the System Administrator — together with the strategic and technical direction recorded in the Project Synopsis.
The document is intended to serve as a formal agreement between the development team and all stakeholders regarding what the system will do, how it will perform, and the constraints under which it will operate. It provides a single authoritative reference for developers, testers, supervisors and evaluators throughout the project lifecycle.
1.2 Scope
The system to be developed is a food delivery marketplace in which restaurants post their full menus to one platform, customers browse meals from many kitchens in a single interface, order instantly, pay by mobile money (or cash on delivery, backed by a rider wallet), and receive delivery through a managed network of motorcycle delivery agents. The feature set mirrors established global delivery platforms, but every layer is redesigned around Cameroonian realities: unreliable street addressing, intermittent connectivity (including a history of internet shutdowns in the South-West), dominant cash-on-delivery habits, mobile-money payment rails and low-end Android devices.
The problem addressed is not a lack of demand but a lack of execution depth. Most food ordering in Cameroon still happens through phone calls and WhatsApp, which is unreliable, unbranded and impossible to scale. The Platform therefore concentrates on two areas that global playbooks handle poorly in Africa: payment collection reliability (especially cash-on-delivery reconciliation) and last-mile address resolution (landmark-based navigation).
Key capabilities of the system include:
Customer registration, authentication, profile and delivery-address management, restaurant discovery, menu browsing, single-restaurant cart management, checkout, payment, order tracking, order history, notifications, reviews, support and favourites.
Merchant account, restaurant, menu, price, inventory, staff, order, payment, sales, customer, promotion, report, notification and availability management.
Delivery agent onboarding with identity verification, online/offline availability, time-boxed dispatch offers, landmark-aware navigation, order lifecycle actions, proof of delivery, wallet-backed cash handling, earnings and support — all usable offline.
Administrator review of merchant and rider applications, user and account management, content moderation, order override and dispute resolution, platform analytics and system configuration, protected by multi-factor authentication and an uneditable audit log.
Idempotent order and payment processing through a payment adapter layer (MTN MoMo and Orange Money) with a reconciliation path for uncertain payment outcomes.
Regulatory compliance: ART/ANTIC certification and NECAP interconnection before the first live transaction.
The initial deployment is deliberately narrow: one neighbourhood corridor in Buea with 10–15 restaurants within a 3 km radius and 5–10 motorcycle riders, with English as the default locale. Expansion to Yaoundé or Douala is out of scope until contribution-margin-positive orders are proven on the Buea corridor.

2. General Description
2.1 Product Functions
The Platform is a two-sided (in fact four-actor) marketplace that connects customers, restaurants and delivery agents under the control of a platform administrator. It fulfils the following primary functions:
Restaurant discovery and menu browsing: Customers find restaurants, search and filter them, and explore menus with options and customisations.
Single-restaurant ordering: Customers build a cart from one restaurant at a time, check out, and pay through supported payment methods.
Order management: Merchants receive, accept or reject, prepare and track orders; customers follow progress in real time.
Managed delivery: Orders are offered to nearby delivery agents, who navigate with landmark notes and phone calls, pick up, deliver and prove delivery.
Payments and cash reconciliation: Mobile money and wallet-backed cash on delivery, with idempotent processing and reconciliation of uncertain payment states.
Earnings and settlement: Rider wallets, withdrawals, merchant payment and sales views, and platform commission tracking.
Verification and trust: Administrator review of merchant and rider documents, moderation, dispute resolution and audit logging.
Offline resilience: The rider app completes pickup, drop-off, proof of delivery and cash collection with zero connectivity and syncs later.
Operations intelligence: Analytics, reports and configurable platform parameters (commission, delivery radius, delivery fee, categories).
The Platform is an operations company that happens to have an app, not an app company hoping operations sort themselves out. Its differentiation must come from reliability, offline durability and wallet trust rather than catalogue size.
2.2 User Community
The Platform serves the following user groups, as identified during stakeholder analysis.
2.2.1 Primary Users – Customers
End users who discover restaurants, explore menus, purchase food and receive fulfilment. They access the Platform through web and mobile applications, select food items, review the cart, choose pickup or delivery, provide delivery information and pay. Their technical knowledge is assumed to be low to moderate. Customers are responsible for providing valid contact and delivery details, including landmark notes.
2.2.2 Primary Users – Merchants and Restaurant Staff
Merchants (restaurant owners or managers) register restaurants, manage menus, prices, inventory, staff, promotions and orders, and monitor payments, sales and reports. Restaurant staff prepare food and process orders under roles assigned by the merchant.
2.2.3 Primary Users – Delivery Agents (Riders)
Motorcycle (and, where permitted, bicycle or car) delivery agents who accept dispatch offers, pick up from restaurants, deliver to customers, handle cash and earn income. They typically use low-end Android devices (1–2 GB RAM), often with gloves and one hand, in variable connectivity. Delivery workers in Buea currently earn roughly 2,000–8,000 FCFA per day (average about 4,000 FCFA), which sets the baseline for the earnings experience.
2.2.4 Primary Users – System Administrators
The system administrators hold superior management control over the entire Platform and act as its regulatory authority, responsible for platform security, integrity, trust and conflict resolution. They work mainly through an administrative web dashboard: they review uploaded files (ID cards, licences, business permits, vehicle registration and other required documents from merchants and riders) and monitor live operational metrics, transaction logs and user-activity data.
2.2.5 Oversight Stakeholders and External Parties
Regulators and compliance bodies (ART – Agence de Régulation des Télécommunications, ANTIC – Agence Nationale des Technologies de l’Information et de la Communication, and the NECAP interconnection for payment and SMS reporting), mobile money operators (MTN MoMo, Orange Money), academic supervisors and the course instructor.

 | 2.5 Ordering Model and Business Rules
The Platform adopts a single-restaurant-per-checkout ordering model. A customer can browse many restaurants, but one cart and one order belong to exactly one restaurant:
A customer selects a restaurant before adding food items to the active shopping cart.
The active cart can contain items from only one restaurant, and a customer cannot combine items from different restaurants in one checkout.
Every successfully submitted order belongs to exactly one restaurant; ordering from another restaurant requires a separate checkout.
If a customer tries to add an item from a different restaurant while the cart contains items, the system displays a warning and asks for confirmation before clearing the cart.
If the customer cancels, the existing cart remains unchanged. If the customer confirms, the cart is cleared and the attempted item from the new restaurant is added.
Both the web and mobile customer applications implement the same fundamental business rules, particularly for cart management, checkout and order creation.

2.6 Launch Context and Buea-Specific Constraints
Launch parameters: Buea, South-West Region; one neighbourhood corridor; 10–15 restaurants within a 3 km radius; 5–10 motorcycle riders; English as the default locale (French also supported); wallet-backed COD so riders never carry unbacked cash risk; unit economics proven per order before any geographic expansion.

 | CT-1
 | 4G exists in Buea town on MTN and Orange, but the Anglophone South-West has a history of internet shutdowns since 2017. Connectivity cannot be assumed as continuously available.

 | 2.8 Assumptions and Dependencies
Confirmed assumptions reflected in this SRS:
The Platform supports multiple restaurants; customers may order from different restaurants but each order requires a separate checkout.
An active cart can contain items from one restaurant only; the system warns the customer and requests confirmation before clearing the existing cart when switching (Option A).
Customers access the system through both web and mobile applications; pickup and delivery are supported where offered by the selected restaurant.
Delivery agents use a dedicated native mobile app; administrators use a web dashboard.
Online payment is the customer-side payment model (see reconciliation note in 2.5 and OD-14).
Dependencies:
Restaurant functionality supplies menu, price, availability and operating-status information to the customer application.
Payment gateway and mobile money services (MTN MoMo, Orange Money) provide verified transaction outcomes.
Notification and SMS providers support OTP, push and customer notifications; they report to NECAP.
Delivery functionality provides delivery progress information to the customer application.
Restaurant service areas and platform rules (delivery radius, commission, base delivery fee configured by the administrator) determine delivery availability and charges.
Map data is incomplete in Buea; the Platform depends on landmark notes, Plus Codes and accumulated pin corrections.

 | 3.3 Data and Validation Rules
3.3.1 Cart Data Constraints
A non-empty active cart shall have exactly one restaurant association.
Every cart item shall reference a menu item belonging to the cart’s associated restaurant.
The system shall reject any cart containing items from multiple restaurants.
Cart items shall retain their item identities, quantities, selected options and relevant price information.
Item availability and prices shall be revalidated before order submission.
3.3.2 Restaurant-Switch Workflow
The customer has an active cart containing items from Restaurant A.
The customer navigates to Restaurant B.
The customer attempts to add an item from Restaurant B to the cart.
The system detects that Restaurant B differs from the restaurant associated with the active cart.
The system displays a warning requesting confirmation to clear the existing cart and switch restaurants.
If the customer cancels, the existing Restaurant A cart remains unchanged.
If the customer confirms, the system clears the old cart.
The system associates the new cart with Restaurant B.
The system adds the attempted Restaurant B item.
The system displays the updated cart.
3.3.3 Order Data Constraints
Every order shall reference exactly one restaurant.
Every order item shall belong to the restaurant referenced by the order.
The order shall preserve the item and price information applicable at the time of submission.
The order shall store the selected fulfilment method.
The order shall contain the necessary pickup or delivery information, including the landmark note for delivery (FR-SYS-06).
The order shall be linked to the customer and the relevant payment transaction.
3.3.4 Delivery Agent Data Constraints
A delivery shall hold at most 50 photos/signatures and 200 barcode records (FR-DEL-6.6).
Every mutating request from the rider app shall carry an idempotency key so that replays after reconnection create no duplicate records (FR-DEL-6.5, FR-SYS-02).
The vehicle type declared at onboarding determines which order types are offered (FR-DEL-1.3).
The corrected pin of a customer’s address is stored against that customer for future orders (FR-DEL-4.5, FR-SYS-07).

 | 5.1 Throughput Targets
Throughput is defined as the volume of location and order events the system processes. The figures below follow directly from the adaptive intervals in PR-07:
Moving rider: one location update every 5 seconds, which equals 12 updates per minute or 720 per hour.
Stopped rider: one update every 30 seconds, which equals 120 per hour.
Idle rider: one update every 60 seconds, which equals 60 per hour.
Offer window: 30 seconds by default, configurable remotely (NFR-62), with delivery of the offer within 2 seconds at the 95th percentile (PR-06).
Data budget: under 5 MB per delivery excluding photo uploads (PR-09); photo and signature uploads are capped at 50 per delivery (FR-DEL-6.6).
Launch volume: 5–10 riders and 10–15 restaurants in a 3 km radius, which is far below the 1,000-rider design capacity in PR-11.
5.2 Scalability
The rider client is stateless with respect to multi-user concurrency; scalability applies primarily to the backend. The system shall:
Start as a modular monolith and be decomposed into services only when order density demands it (DC-01).
Use Redis for geospatial indexing and idempotency, PostgreSQL for orders and payments, and a queue for event fan-out (DC-02) so that growth does not require redesign.
Scale rider-location handling linearly from 1,000 to 10,000 concurrent riders (PR-11).
Allow additional payment, notification and map providers without a complete redesign (NFR-58).
Degrade gracefully when the backend is temporarily unavailable: clients queue and retry rather than fail hard (NFR-25).
